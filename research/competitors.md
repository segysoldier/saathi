# Saathi: Competitor Deep-Dive (as of Nov 2026)

## Summary
- The "tired moment" is already owned by story apps. Pocket FM reports about $500M annualised revenue and 250M+ listeners (company claims, via TechCrunch). Kuku TV and other micro-drama apps sell ended, serialised, cliffhanger content. Saathi competes with these, not with blockers.
- Blockers and friction apps (one sec, ScreenZen, Opal, StayFree) sell friction or tracking. None found offers a replacement activity tied to the user's exam or career goal.
- PW's Gyaan-E is the closest big-brand analogue: 2-3 minute vertical lessons at Rs 749/year. It is a catalogue of skill courses (AI, finance, editing), not an exam-world rest experience. It is the most direct incumbent threat if PW chooses to add stories.
- Micro-drama economics are unproven. Pocket FM shut Pocket TV in June 2026 to refocus on audio, and few players disclose profitability. Duolingo shows streak mechanics drive daily use (64.9M DAU, up 29%) but converts slowly (13.1M paid, up 14%).
- Willingness to pay among 18-24 is thin and price-sensitive. UPI AutoPay works for small recurring amounts (standard cap Rs 15,000). Use pay-per-pass or a cheap monthly plan. No source isolates students on price.

## Is the gap real?
Partly, and the gap is narrower than the pitch suggests.

**What is real.** I found no product that offers a short, ended, story-led rest in the world of the user's exam goal at the moment they are about to scroll. Blockers add friction but no substitute. Study-reel apps still feed more content and optimise for study time. Story apps entertain but are not goal-aligned.

**What is not proven.**
- Nothing here shows that an 18-24 user will choose a 5-minute episode over Reels at the tired moment. Pocket FM's own data points to 5-15 minute episodes preferred by 40% of users, so length fits, but that is from a survey of its own users, not about replacing Reels.
- There is no evidence of anyone charging for this. I found no independent evidence that study-reel apps reduce doomscrolling.
- Distribution is the real obstacle. A Telegram bot only works if the user opens it. The Android pause layer is the actual product, and it needs permissions and trust.
- Gyaan-E and Pocket FM could build this quickly. A moat has to be story quality and the specific exam world, not the format.

**Verdict:** a real white space, not yet a proven market. Test cheaply: run the Telegram pack with 50-100 users in one exam niche (NEET first) and track whether they open it instead of Reels, measured by return rate on day 7.

## Pricing recommendation
Evidence is thin, so treat these as hypotheses to test.
- Free: one episode a day, with the question and task. Keep the ritual free. Pocket FM and micro-drama apps use free-first, then pay.
- Paid: a low monthly or quarterly pass. Rs 49-99/month, or about Rs 249-299/quarter, sits below Kuku's Rs 399/quarter and Gyaan-E's Rs 749/year. This is my inference, not a tested price point. Lumikai's 2025 report reportedly shows users prefer monthly plans over annual (excerpt only). A "season pass" per serial is an alternative.
- Collect via UPI AutoPay mandate. Standard cap is Rs 15,000 per debit, far above these prices. Keep one-tap cancel. Gyaan-E's no-auto-renew and 7-day refund show the trust bar for young buyers.
- Avoid anything near Opal-level pricing. Test one price per cohort rather than trusting the figures above.
